    Continuous fault observations

    The chaos VM gate records traffic before each fault is injected and keeps sampling throughout the watchdog response, the existing recovery audits and a final three-second hold. The old 18-second wait remains a recovery deadline; it no longer removes that interval from the external observation.

    What is measured

    An ordinary process with its own UID/GID, no supplementary groups and no effective capabilities attempts TCP/80, DNS UDP/53 and arbitrary UDP/19302 in both IPv4 and IPv6. Its controlled destinations are 198.51.100.7 and 2001:db8:5444::7, outside the default LAN exemptions. No public response is needed for these egress observations. Real Tor/public-IP recovery checks remain separate, and an unavailable public dependency still fails its own audit.

    The process starts before TTP and continues across injections and session resets. Every cycle has a sequence number; datagrams carry a unique run/channel payload and TCP attempts record their source ports. The nominal interval is 100 ms for each channel. The report records the largest sampled gap and fails if any channel has a gap over 500 ms in a fault interval. This is sampled observation: a shorter unsampled opening can still be missed.

    QEMU records complete Ethernet frames outside the guest firewall, before user-network NAT. A missing/corrupt/truncated capture, unsupported required packet layout, missing sequence/channel or lost phase beacon cannot yield a clean result. Unlike a host AF_PACKET socket, this observer has no kernel packet socket receive queue; a write/read failure or missing required data invalidates the evidence. The capture is streamed during analysis.

    Root emits distinct non-IP Ethernet phase beacons around injection commands, controls and hold intervals. These create no IP bypass for the ordinary actor. The analyser requires the same ordered beacons in the independent capture and the guest log. It uses capture order for phases and QEMU timestamps for packet exposure bounds; it never subtracts a guest clock from a host/QEMU clock. Successful unprotected controls must expose all six channels before and after the sweep. The same controls also run before each reset session.

    After a link flap, IPv6 address discovery and duplicate-address detection can still be pending when the intentional stop completes. Before unprotected controls, the harness waits at most 20 seconds for both routes and a usable non-tentative IPv6 source address. It records the chosen paths, changes no routes or addresses, and keeps the stimulus/capture running throughout. A missing family still fails; this wait does not shorten any fault window. After a confirmed intentional stop it sends a scoped IPv6 router solicitation to the VM's actual local router, since earlier discovery attempts may have been blocked during the fault. This is calibration after release, and does not establish automatic IPv6 availability recovery during protection. Client sockets use outbound connect with implicit local address selection, without binding a wildcard address or opening a listener.

    Containment and recovery are separate results

    Fault family Required result
    Tor orderly stop/SIGKILL, resolver overlay removal, link flap No observed direct probe packet in the fault window; recovery audits also pass
    Privileged insertion of an accept rule, deletion of the final reject rule, table flush/destruction Record every observed escaped packet as a leak/exposure; require the final hold to contain traffic and recovery audits to pass
    Missing calibration, stimulus, capture or planned injection Inconclusive/harness failure

    Removing the enforcement barrier as root can expose traffic before the watchdog repairs or isolates it. An accepted recovery experiment for that deliberate mutation does not mean continuous containment. Its JSON keeps containment: leak, packet/byte counts and first/last observed egress. Exposure from an ordinary non-firewall fault fails the gate, even when it later recovers. Any direct probe packet during the final hold fails, including after a privileged enforcement mutation.

    A positively matched packet bracketed by known fault beacons remains a leak even if later capture data is lost. Such incomplete experiments still fail; unprotected calibration packets alone cannot establish a fault leak.

    accepted means that the experiment met the row's stated expectations. It is not a blanket security verdict. No new runtime barrier or instantaneous recovery guarantee is introduced by this test harness.

    Running and finding evidence

    Use the disposable VM runner, never your normal host:

    VM_WORK=/path/to/new-private-workdir scripts/vm/lifecycle/chaos.sh
VM_WORK=/path/to/another-workdir \
  CHAOS_ARGS="--reset-between --no-bypass --duration 2400" \
  scripts/vm/lifecycle/chaos.sh

    The lifecycle workflow covers chained, fresh-session and fresh-session/no-bypass variants. --injection kill_tor selects a single fault for local reproduction. The wrapper's exit status requires both recovery audits and external evidence to succeed. It retains chaos.pcap, chaos.log, chaos-boundary/events.jsonl, chaos-boundary/stimulus.jsonl and chaos-boundary-report.json; CI uploads them. Captures may include unrelated guest/Tor traffic and are diagnostic artifacts.

    These tests cover the stated synthetic actor, interface and packet shapes. They do not qualify browsers, physical handover/suspend, every transport, full startup/rollback/teardown or interfaces outside the observed NIC. See DNS/UDP evidence for the separate steady-session fixture.
